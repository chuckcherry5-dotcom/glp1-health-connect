Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide: A Complete Comparison

Last updated: August 2026 | Sourced from FDA and NIH publications

If you're considering a GLP-1 telehealth program, you'll likely encounter two medications: semaglutide and tirzepatide. While both are effective for weight management and blood sugar control, they have key differences that may affect which is right for you.

What Are These Medications?

Semaglutide (brand names: Ozempic®, Wegovy®) is a GLP-1 receptor agonist—a medication that mimics glucagon-like peptide-1, a hormone that regulates appetite and blood sugar.

Tirzepatide (brand name: Mounjaro®) is a GLP-1 and GIP receptor agonist—it targets both GLP-1 and glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide (GIP) receptors, making it a "dual agonist."

FDA Source: Both medications are FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related conditions. Tirzepatide was approved for weight management in November 2023. Semaglutide was approved for weight management in June 2021.

How They Work (Mechanism of Action)

Semaglutide

Semaglutide works by:

  • Stimulating GLP-1 receptors in the brain to reduce appetite
  • Slowing gastric emptying (the stomach empties more slowly)
  • Improving insulin secretion
  • Regulating blood sugar levels

Tirzepatide

Tirzepatide has an additional mechanism—it also activates GIP receptors, which:

  • Enhances insulin secretion in response to meals
  • Reduces glucagon release (which raises blood sugar)
  • May provide additional weight loss benefits compared to GLP-1 alone
Clinical Evidence: Clinical trials show tirzepatide produces greater weight loss than semaglutide. However, individual responses vary, and the "best" medication depends on your specific health profile and how your body responds.

Efficacy: Weight Loss Comparison

Medication Maximum Dose Average Weight Loss Study Duration
Semaglutide (Wegovy) 2.4 mg weekly ~18% body weight 68 weeks
Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) 15 mg weekly ~22% body weight 72 weeks

In clinical trials, tirzepatide demonstrated greater weight loss compared to semaglutide. However, both medications are significantly more effective than lifestyle changes alone for people with obesity.

Common Side Effects

Both Medications May Cause:

  • Nausea (especially when starting or increasing doses)
  • Vomiting
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Stomach pain or cramping
  • Fatigue
  • Dizziness

Side Effects That May Differ:

  • Semaglutide: Injection site reactions at the point where you inject the pen
  • Tirzepatide: May have a higher rate of gastrointestinal side effects, particularly nausea, compared to semaglutide in some studies

Important: Side effects are typically mild and often decrease over time as your body adjusts to the medication. Your healthcare provider can help manage any side effects through dose adjustments or additional support.

Serious Warnings and Contraindications

FDA Warning: Both medications carry a black box warning about the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors (based on animal studies). They should not be used by people with a personal history of medullary thyroid cancer or a family history of multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2).

Other Important Warnings:

  • Pancreatitis risk: Rare cases of acute pancreatitis have been reported. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience severe abdominal pain.
  • Diabetic retinopathy: In people with type 2 diabetes, rapid blood sugar improvement can worsen existing diabetic eye disease.
  • Gallbladder issues: Rapid weight loss can increase the risk of gallstones.
  • Pregnancy: Both medications may harm a developing fetus. Use effective contraception and inform your provider if you're planning to become pregnant.

Dosing and Administration

Medication Administration Starting Dose Maintenance Dose
Semaglutide Subcutaneous injection once weekly 0.25 mg 2.4 mg
Tirzepatide Subcutaneous injection once weekly 2.5 mg 15 mg

Both medications are injected once per week, which many patients find convenient compared to daily medications. Doses are gradually increased over several weeks to minimize side effects and allow your body to adjust.

Cost and Insurance Coverage

Both medications can be expensive, often ranging from $1,000–$1,500+ per month without insurance or savings programs. Insurance coverage varies significantly by plan and whether you're using the medication for weight management (off-label) versus type 2 diabetes (FDA-approved indication).

Most telehealth programs have partnerships with pharmacies and offer patient assistance programs to help reduce costs.

Which One Might Be Right for You?

Choosing between semaglutide and tirzepatide depends on several factors:

Consider Semaglutide If:

  • You're new to GLP-1 medications and want to start with an established option
  • You have a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer (tirzepatide is also contraindicated, but semaglutide has been in use longer)
  • You prefer a medication that's been used for weight management since 2021
  • You experience significant nausea with other medications and want to start slower

Consider Tirzepatide If:

  • You're looking for potentially greater weight loss (clinical data suggests higher average results)
  • You have type 2 diabetes in addition to weight management goals
  • You've tried semaglutide without adequate results
  • You tolerate gastrointestinal side effects relatively well

The Bottom Line

Both semaglutide and tirzepatide are effective medications for weight management. Tirzepatide may offer slightly greater weight loss on average, but semaglutide has been used for longer. The "best" choice depends on your individual health profile, how your body responds, and your healthcare provider's recommendation.

Many people on a telehealth program start with one medication and, with their provider's guidance, may switch to the other if they don't achieve desired results or experience problematic side effects.

Always discuss your options with your telehealth provider to determine which medication is most appropriate for you.
